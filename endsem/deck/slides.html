Problem statement

Agreement Meets Confidence: A Training-Free Fusion That Outperforms EigenScore

LLMs hallucinate confidently

A wrong answer is written in the same fluent, assertive tone as a correct one, so the text alone gives no warning.

No answer key at run time

A deployed detector must flag likely-wrong answers using only the model itself, without the correct answer.

Q: In which year did the bridge open?   Model: “The bridge opened in 1928.”  Confident, fluent, and wrong.

Goal: give every answer a hallucination score, so that unreliable answers can be flagged before they reach the user.

Existing work

EigenScore, our reproduction, and what it cannot see

Sample K = 10 answers, read each answer's last-token state at the middle layer, and score the log-volume of the cloud:

ES = (1/K) · Σ log λᵢ(Σ + αI)

Midsem reproduction (250 CoQA questions, 8 GB GPU): EigenScore 83.5 AUROC, best of five methods, matching the paper.

Problem we address
  • One kind of evidence. It only measures how far the answers spread inside the network.
  • Blind to repeated mistakes. If the model gives the same wrong answer ten times, the spread is zero and EigenScore trusts it.
  • Ignores confidence that the model reports for every word it writes.

Our idea

Three kinds of evidence, already inside the same 10 samples

Internal

EigenScore

How far the answers spread in the network's hidden states.

Surface

Text agreement

How much the 10 answers overlap in their words (mean pairwise ROUGE-L).

Token

Token confidence

How sure the model was of each word, averaged per answer (LN-Entropy).

Key question: do these signals fail on different questions? If so, combining them should beat any one of them, at no extra cost: no new generations and no training.

Proposed method

TRI: rank fusion of the three signals

TRI = ⅓ [ rank(ES) + rank(Agreement) + rank(Confidence) ]
  • Ranks, not raw values: the three scores live on different scales, ranks make them comparable
  • Equal weights, no tuning, no labels: nothing is fitted to the data
  • Higher TRI = more likely a hallucination
Companion method · CTC

Consistency-then-confidence: rank by text agreement, and only where it ties (often all 10 answers identical) rank by token confidence.

Ties get half credit in AUROC, so breaking them with an informative signal can only help.

Why it works

The signals make different mistakes

Only partly correlated

Rank correlation between signals: 0.62 – 0.90. Internal spread and text agreement overlap most; token confidence is the most independent.

Confidence sees repeated mistakes

Among questions where all answers agree, token confidence still separates right from wrong (within-tie AUC 0.65 – 0.73 across datasets; EigenScore only 0.59 on CoQA).

Ablation on the hardest dataset (NQ-Open, model accuracy 27%): dropping text agreement or token confidence costs TRI over 1 point; dropping EigenScore costs 0.6. On CoQA and TriviaQA, agreement plus confidence alone does as well as TRI.

Experimental protocol

Develop on one dataset, confirm on two unseen ones

Setup

  • Llama-2-7B, 4-bit, RTX 4060 (8 GB)
  • 10 samples per question
  • 1,000 questions per dataset

Development

  • CoQA only
  • Ideas explored here
  • Final methods frozen in git

Held-out test

  • TriviaQA, NQ-Open
  • Analysed only after freezing
  • Paired bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, Holm correction

Label: an answer is a hallucination when its ROUGE-L with the reference is at most 0.5.

Conclusion

Combine agreement with confidence

  • A label-free fusion of internal, surface and token signals beats EigenScore by 2.7 – 4.5 AUROC on three datasets
  • Gains held on two datasets frozen out of development
  • EigenScore's layer choice barely matters, and averaging layers, normalising embeddings or tracking cross-layer structure did not help

Limitations and next steps

  • One 7B model, 4-bit; ROUGE labels can be noisy
  • Next: a second model and LLM-judge labels
