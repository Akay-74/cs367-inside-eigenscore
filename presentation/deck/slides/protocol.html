How detectors are compared

MethodSignal it uses
PerplexityAverage token log-probability
LN-EntropyLength-normalised entropy over K answers
EnergyLogit energy, an out-of-distribution score
Lexical SimilarityMean pairwise ROUGE-L of the K answers
EigenScoreCovariance of K middle-layer embeddings

Metric

AUROC

Probability that a wrong answer scores higher than a right one. 50 = chance.

Ground truth

ROUGE-L > 0.5

An answer counts as correct if it overlaps the reference enough (AUC_r).

[Speaker: Shlok · about 35 s] To evaluate the method, the paper compares EigenScore against four baselines: perplexity, length-normalised entropy, the energy score, and lexical similarity. Each method produces a score for every answer. An answer is labelled correct if its ROUGE-L overlap with the reference exceeds 0.5. The methods are compared by AUROC: the probability that a randomly chosen wrong answer receives a higher score than a randomly chosen correct one. An AUROC of 50 is chance level, and 100 is a perfect detector. (Note: this slide can also be delivered by Prapti if you want a more even split of speaking time.)
