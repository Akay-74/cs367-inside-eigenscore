Why a log-determinant

It is an entropy

For a Gaussian, log det Σ is the differential entropy up to a constant. EigenScore is semantic uncertainty measured in embedding space.

It is cheap

Σ is 10 × 10, not 4096 × 4096. The extra cost is negligible next to generating the K answers.

It sees meaning

Two answers with different words but the same meaning get similar embeddings; word-overlap metrics would call them different.

[Speaker: Shlok · about 35 s] There are three reasons for this choice of score. First, for a Gaussian distribution the log-determinant of the covariance is, up to a constant, its differential entropy, so EigenScore is a measure of semantic uncertainty in embedding space. Second, it is cheap: the matrix is only ten by ten, because we compare the answers with each other rather than the 4096 features. Third, it captures meaning rather than wording: two answers that use different words for the same fact obtain similar embeddings, whereas a word-overlap metric would treat them as different.
