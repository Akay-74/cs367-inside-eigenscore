2 · Method

EigenScore: spread measured by eigenvalues

Σ = Zᵀ J Z ∈ ℝ^(K×K)

E = (1/K) · log det(Σ + αI)

= (1/K) · Σᵢ log λᵢ

Z holds the K answer embeddings as columns, J centres them, and α = 10⁻³ keeps the matrix invertible.

Each eigenvalue λᵢ is the variance along one direction of disagreement.

Confident. Answers agree; eigenvalues fall to the floor α. Low score.

Guessing. Answers diverge; eigenvalues stay large. High score.

[Speaker: Shlok · about 45 s] Formally, we stack the K embeddings as the columns of a matrix Z, centre them, and form the K-by-K covariance matrix sigma. The EigenScore is one over K times the log-determinant of sigma plus a small multiple of the identity, which equals the average log-eigenvalue. Each eigenvalue is the variance of the answers along one direction. On the left, the model is confident: the ten embeddings almost coincide, so nearly all eigenvalues fall to the floor set by alpha, and the score is low. On the right, the model is guessing: the embeddings spread out in several directions, the eigenvalues stay large, and the score is high.
