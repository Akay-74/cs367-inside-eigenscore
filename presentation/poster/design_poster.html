LLM Hallucination Detection using Multi-Layer EigenScore

Catching an AI that is confidently wrong, using only what happens inside the model.
Problem statement

Large language models often produce fluent, confident answers that are factually wrong (hallucinations), in the same tone as correct ones.

We need a detector that flags these answers without knowing the right answer, without external fact-checking, and that runs on an ordinary 8 GB laptop GPU.

How we solved it

1

Ask again

Sample 10 answers to the same question at moderate randomness.

2

Look inside

For each answer, read the model’s internal state: middle layers, last token, 4096 numbers.

3

Measure spread

Covariance of the 10 answers → eigenvalues → one EigenScore. High = likely wrong.

4

Calm overconfidence

Feature clipping: cap rare extreme activations so confident errors start to vary.

5

Fit a laptop

4-bit model, one-layer capture, 8 automated tests: runs on an 8 GB GPU.

Spread in meaning = uncertainty

EigenScore = mean( log λi ), λ = eig( Cov(answers) + αI )

Multi-Layer EigenScore averages this score over a band of middle layers (8–24 of 32) instead of one hand-picked layer, for a steadier, model-independent signal.

Feature clipping during generation

Each neuron of the penultimate layer is capped to its 0.2–99.8 percentile range, estimated from the last 3,000 tokens. Extreme “shouting” values (coral) are pulled in, reducing over-confident generation.
